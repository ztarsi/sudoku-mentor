# UX review, 29 October 2026

Reviewed by the product manager on the live staging build at https://sdm.pilia.net, driven at 1440x900, 1366x768 and 390x844. Three independent passes were taken over the same captures: a first-time-player pass, a teaching-surface pass, and an accessibility pass. Everything below that is stated as fact was measured in the running app, not read off a screenshot.

Prompted by the founder, 29 October, on seeing the Scan dialog: "This is bad UI, you should know better than to create this garbage." He is right, and the cause is traced in finding 1.

Judged against Nielsen's ten heuristics, the quality bar in `docs/PRODUCT.md` section 8, and WCAG for the measurable parts.

## What I checked and disproved

Two findings from the review passes did not survive measurement, and are recorded here so nobody acts on them.

- **"The hint card's Apply and Next hint fall below the fold at 1366x768."** False. Measured: the buttons sit at 717 to 753 pixels in a 768-pixel viewport, fully visible. The screenshot that suggested otherwise was misread.
- **"A first-time visitor lands in the middle of someone else's game."** False. A fresh browser context lands on a fresh puzzle; the "31% complete" it shows is the givens, counted as progress. The real problem is the metric, which is finding 5.

The habit of checking is the point. Two of the three worst-sounding findings were artefacts, and the fourth-worst turned out to be real.

## Findings

### 1. Scan is a progress animation pretending to be a result

**What a player gets.** They ask what is on this board. They wait four seconds, watching thirty-three rows tick over one at a time, each labelled "instant". The board is covered while this happens. At the end they scroll three and a half screens through twenty-one rows saying "0 found" to locate the twelve that matched, under a heading that still reads "Scanning Ultimate Techniques".

**Measured on staging.**

| | Desktop | Laptop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rows | 33 | 33 |
| Rows reading "0 found" | 21 | 21 |
| Screens of scrolling | 3.5 | 4.1 |
| Wall clock | 4.0 s | 4.5 s |
| Board visible | No | No |
| Heading when finished | "Scanning Ultimate Techniques" | same |

**Of that time, 3.3 seconds is deliberate.** `LogicPanel.jsx` pauses 100 ms per technique with the comment "Small delay for UI update". The real work is about 0.7 seconds. The product spends three seconds of the player's time animating a list so it looks busy.

**Heuristics broken:** visibility of system status (the status is theatre, not state), aesthetic and minimalist design, user control and freedom, and consistency between what the product says and what it does.

**It also contradicts itself twice.** Every row says "instant" during a four-second wait. The Ultimate tier's own note promises Scan "takes a second or two".

**Why it exists, which is the part worth learning.** This dialog is a progress indicator from when the Ultimate tier held five techniques. It was never redesigned when the tier grew to thirty-three. My own panel-at-scale spec redesigned the technique list for a 55-technique catalog and said of Scan only that it "says what it is about to do". I specified the announcement and never the result, so a five-technique component survived into a sixty-one-technique product. Tests cannot see a wall of zeroes, so nothing caught it.

**What it should be.** No modal. Press Scan and the rows in the Scan tiers show that they are counting; the counts fill in; the techniques with instances join the "on this board" section at the top of the panel, where the live-counted ones already are. One line of progress with a cancel, not thirty-three rows. Nothing found says so in one line rather than twenty-one. The board stays visible throughout, because the counts are about the board. Drop the artificial delay and the per-detector time labels.

### 2. Undo is lost on reload, though the board is not

**Measured.** Place a digit: progress goes 31% to 32% and Undo becomes available. Reload: the board is still at 32%, and Undo is disabled again.

The quality bar says "Nothing the player did is lost by a reload, a tab switch, or closing the app." The work is not lost. The ability to take it back is. A player who returns to a puzzle and spots a mistake they made before the reload cannot step back to it.

This is narrower than "the game is lost" and still a miss against a bar we wrote down. Either the history is persisted with the board, or the bar should say that it is not.

### 3. Touch targets are below the minimum, and one of them matters

Measured on the phone viewport:

| Control | Size | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding dismiss ("Got it" ×) | 22 × 22 | 44 × 44 |
| Hint button | 81 × 36 | 44 high |
| Pencil, Marks, Undo, Redo, Erase | 66 × 40 | 44 high |
| Digit keys on phone | 40 × 40 | 44 × 44 |

The 22-pixel dismiss is the one that matters: it is half the minimum, it sits over the board, and it is the only way to clear the coach mark. The others are near-misses that a thumb will mostly hit. The digit keys at 40 are a deliberate trade for fitting the strip, and the spec's own test asserts 44; that disagreement should be settled rather than left.

### 4. The digit keys carry unexplained superscripts

The strip shows 1⁴ 2³ 3¹ 4³ 5² 6⁵ 7² 8³ 9². Nothing says whether that is how many are placed or how many remain, and on the phone a key reads 1⁰, which is ambiguous either way. The digit-strip spec asked for "digits with counts" and I never said counts of what, or that the player would need telling.

### 5. "31% complete" counts the givens

A fresh puzzle announces that the player is nearly a third done before they have touched it. Progress should measure what the player has done, or the label should say what it is counting.

### 6. The library sells puzzles in vocabulary the player has not met

Rows read "Teaches up to Naked Single" and "Teaches up to ALS-XZ" to someone who has never seen either. The measured labels were a real gain and they are written for us, not for a beginner choosing a puzzle. The quality bar says explanations use no undefined vocabulary.

Related and separate: the Easy shelf holds two 17-clue puzzles, "Nightmare" and "X-Wing Territory". By technique they are easy, which is what the shelf measures. To a beginner, a grid with 17 givens does not look easy, and one of them is named for a technique it never teaches, which is already filed as #79.

### 7. Smaller things, each real

- **The desktop coach mark says "Tap a cell, then a digit"** on a 1440-pixel window driven by a mouse. The phone's wording is right for a phone; the desktop's is not.
- **The padlock on the library's Image tab explains nothing.** On staging it can never unlock, since there is no platform behind it.
- **On the phone the library's third tab is clipped to "Tex".** Text import is one of the few routes staging actually supports.
- **The phone truncates the puzzle name to "Brai…".**

## What is good, and should not be touched

Nothing scrolls horizontally and nothing falls below the fold at any of the three widths. The hint card reads well: technique, tier, Look, Why it works, What to do, a legend naming a real cell, and two clear actions. The menu is complete and identical across widths. The "on this board" section of the Techniques panel is the right idea and survives a 61-technique catalog. The Ultimate tier's explanation of why those detectors are not counted live is honest, well-written copy; only its timing claim is wrong.
