# UX/UI review: 18 September 2026

A full run-through of the experience on desktops, laptops, tablets and phones. The founder's brief: nothing has to stay as it is; make the experience the best it can be for each of those. This document is the audit and a proposed target experience. It is not a spec; specs follow once the founder has answered the decisions at the end.

Build reviewed: `main` at `57c1583`, which includes the eight fix batches from the [code review](2026-09-18-code-review.md). Evidence images are in `images/ux-2026-09-18/`.

## 1. The screen-size question

The founder asked whether 1366x768 is really a standard size. Two answers.

**What the world uses.** By the public StatCounter figures I know (worth re-checking, I could not reach the site from the review environment), 1920x1080 is the most common desktop screen worldwide at roughly a quarter of visits, and 1366x768 is second at roughly a sixth. Most budget and business laptops sold in the last ten years are 1366x768. Two other sizes matter: 1536x864, which is a 1920x1080 laptop running Windows at the default 125% zoom, and 1440x900 or 1470x956, which is a 13-inch MacBook Air.

**What the page actually gets.** Browsers take space. A 1920x1080 monitor with a tab bar, address bar, bookmarks bar and the Windows taskbar leaves the page about 950 pixels tall. A 13-inch MacBook leaves about 780. The founder's screen is probably large or high-resolution, so the page has always had room. Most players' pages do not.

The measurements from the walkthrough: after pressing Hint, at 1366x768 the explanation is entirely below the fold; at 1536x864 it starts below the fold; at 1920x1080 the "What to do" section is below the fold. See `laptop-1366x768-after-hint.jpg` and `desktop-1536-full-page-after-hint.jpg`.

## 2. What was reviewed and how

Scripted walkthroughs in Chromium on the local build, plus screenshots read by eye.

| Surface | Sizes | Input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop and laptop page | 1920x1080, 1536x864, 1440x900, 1366x900, 1366x768, 1280x720 | Mouse and keyboard |
| Narrow window of the desktop page | 900x800 | Mouse |
| Tablet, landscape (desktop page) | 1080x810 (iPad) | Touch |
| Tablet, portrait (mobile page) | 810x1080 (iPad) | Touch |
| Phone | 390x664 (iPhone 13), 360x640, 844x390 landscape | Touch |

Every screen and state I could reach without an account: first visit and tour, play, wrong entry, hints in both explanation levels, what-if search, Scan, the technique reference, the hierarchy, Focus Mode, candidates on and off, Auto-Solve, No Assist, Load Puzzle (library, text, the locked image tab), colour settings, keyboard shortcuts, completion, print, toasts, reload. Not reviewed: sign-in, the account menu, photo import, saved puzzles and best times.

## 3. The five conclusions

1. **The layout is built around the technique hierarchy, not the lesson.** On every desktop size the hierarchy, a 540-pixel expert browser, sits above the hint card. The lesson, which is the product, is the third thing on the page and off-screen for most players when it appears.
2. **The product has three different experiences, and only one of them teaches.** The wide desktop page teaches. The narrow desktop page hides the lesson in a drawer. The mobile page, which tablets in portrait also get, has no hints, no pencil marks on load, and no technique reference. A tablet in landscape gets the desktop page with touch, where the Hint button is off-screen and there is no keyboard to press H.
3. **Chrome competes with the board.** Seven unlabelled icon buttons in the header, a Focus Mode card, an Auto-Solve card and a Keyboard Shortcuts card all sit at the same visual weight as the hint. The principle in PRODUCT.md is "the board is the hero, chrome disappears when it has done its job". Today nothing disappears.
4. **The hint card has no designed states.** Idle, searching, found, what-if, No Assist, solved and "nothing left to find" all render into the same card with the same green "Technique Found!" heading or a lightbulb. The what-if case is the honesty promise and it looks like every other hint.
5. **Input works differently on each surface for no player reason.** Desktop: select a cell, type. Phone: pick a digit, tap cells. Desktop Focus Mode is a separate card with the same nine digits the phone uses for input. One digit strip could do both jobs everywhere.

## 4. Findings by surface

### A. Desktop and laptop (mouse, 1024px and wider)

**A1. Header.** Left: logo, name, puzzle name, shelf. Right: progress pill (only above 1280px), then seven icon-only buttons (colours, about, No Assist, candidates, print, copy, load), then Sign in. Icons have tooltips and screen-reader names, but a new player has to hover each one to learn what it does. The No Assist toggle, a mode that changes what the whole product does, is a lightbulb icon with no state text. Print and copy are utilities that do not need a place in the top bar.

**A2. Board and Focus Mode.** The board is well drawn: givens white, player digits blue, candidates readable, the cells of a hint clearly coloured. Focus Mode is a full-width card under the board with nine buttons and placed-count badges. It is useful (see where a digit can still go) but its card is as heavy as the board's and it pushes the fold down. On the phone the same nine digits are the input bar.

**A3. Right column order.** Technique Hierarchy (expanded by default, 540px), then the hint card, then Auto-Solve, then Keyboard Shortcuts. Only the hierarchy's header is a sensible first thing for the enthusiast; for the improver and learner the hint is what they came for. At 1366x768 the lesson is fully below the fold. At 1536x864 the column is 1119px tall on an 864px window.

**A4. Hint card states.** Idle shows "Ready for a Hint?" with a lightbulb button. Found shows "Technique Found!" with the tier, a green technique pill, a "Digit: N" pill (wrong for pairs and triples), the Beginner/Expert switch, then Look / Why / What to do, then a fixed legend "R5C3 means row 5, column 3", then eliminations as red chips. What-if shows the same "Technique Found!" heading with an amber note underneath. Searching (new in batch 6) shows a spinner and Cancel. Solved shows nothing about what was learned. There is no "next hint" or "I found it myself" action on the card; the player presses H again.

**A5. Dialogs.** Load Puzzle: tabs Library, Image (locked), Text; six shelf pills; a list of puzzles with a tiny 16-pixel preview, name, clue count, and Play. No sense of which puzzles teach what, which are done, or where the player left off. Technique reference: good structure (plain words, formal, how to find it, example), plain black text, one primary button. No Assist: a long explanation of what is disabled, then Cancel / Enable. About: marketing text. Colour settings: five targets, eighteen swatches, a hex field, three saveable presets. This is a large feature for a small need and it is the first icon in the header.

**A6. Tour.** A three-step modal on first visit that blocks the board. Correct content, but a player cannot touch the board until they dismiss it, and it repeats the same information the hint card could show in context.

**A7. Completion.** Confetti, "Puzzle Solved!", time, error count, "Perfect Solve!", Continue. It does not say which techniques the puzzle used or which the player learned, and offers no next puzzle. The time is shown even for hint-assisted solves, which reads like a record.

**A8. Toasts.** Now auto-dismiss (batch 1). They still stack over the top rows of the board at the top centre. "Resumed your puzzle" on every return visit is noise.

**A9. Auto-Solve.** A card with Play, Step, and four speeds, always visible. PRODUCT.md lists automatic solving as a non-goal for players and says playback exists to demonstrate reasoning. Its placement says otherwise.

**A10. Keyboard shortcuts.** A collapsed card. Fine, but it is a reference, not a control, and does not belong in the main column.

### B. Narrow window (mouse, under 1024px)

The desktop page switches to a bottom bar (Hint, Apply, Undo, Redo, Clear, Logic). Pressing Hint colours the board and nothing else: the explanation lives in a drawer behind "Logic", and inside the drawer the hierarchy again sits above the hint card. Toasts cover the top of the board. See `narrow-window-900-after-hint.jpg`. Clear (wipe the whole board) sits next to Undo in the thumb row with only its red colour as a warning.

### C. Tablet

**Landscape (1080x810, touch).** Gets the desktop page. The board fills the left, the hierarchy fills the right, and the Hint button is the lightbulb below the hierarchy, off-screen. There is no keyboard for H. Header icons are 40px, acceptable. See `ipad-landscape-after-hint.jpg`.

**Portrait (810x1080, touch).** Gets the mobile page. The board is 783px wide, the nine digit buttons are 36px squares spread across the full width with 60px gaps, the mode buttons stretch to 640px, and there are 300px of empty space under the board. No hints, no pencil marks on load. This is the largest touch screen and the least capable experience. See `ipad-portrait.jpg`.

### D. Phone

**Good.** The board fits with no scrolling at 390x664 and 360x640. Digit-first input is right for thumbs. The strip "21% complete / No errors" is exactly the quiet status the desktop lacks. Pencil-mark pad now stacks above the mode row and names the cell (batch 5). Wrong entries flash and count. Reload restores. Landscape is usable.

**Not good.**
- No hints, no technique reference, no pencil marks on load, no what-if. The phone cannot teach. The roadmap already says this must change.
- The red badge in the header is No Assist mode with no words; nothing explains that every phone solve is timed and counted as a clean solve.
- Digit buttons are 36px, below the usual 44px minimum; cells are 40px.
- After a wrong digit the digit stays selected, so the next tap repeats the mistake. That is consistent with digit-first input, but a learner racks up errors without understanding why.
- Erase is a small icon; Undo is a small icon; Clear (wipe) is on a long-press menu that the tour mentions but nothing on screen hints at.
- The tour says "Long-press a cell for more options" but does not say what they are.
- Colour settings and Load Puzzle are the only header actions, both icon-only. The library dialog is the desktop one squeezed into the phone; the Text tab's textarea placeholder shows a format tutorial in monospace.

### E. What is good and should stay

- The board itself: proportions, digit weights, the candidate layout, and the hint colouring (blue for pattern cells, red for cells that lose a candidate, amber for the digit to place).
- Beginner explanations in Look / Why / What to do, and the Expert level with its glossary.
- The technique reference dialog's four-part structure.
- Digit-first input on touch.
- The phone's status strip.
- Keyboard coverage on desktop.
- The dark palette works for the board; it is the chrome around it that is heavy.

## 5. Proposed target experience

### 5.1 Principles for the redesign

1. **Board and lesson, nothing else at first glance.** Everything that is not the board or the current lesson is a level down: behind a menu, a tab, or a sheet.
2. **One product, one layout system.** The same page adapts by width and by pointer type. There is no separate "mobile page" and no surface where hints do not exist.
3. **The lesson is always on screen when it fires.** On every width the explanation appears where the eye is, without scrolling, with the board still visible.
4. **One input model, two habits.** A single digit strip under the board on every device. Tap a digit then cells (digit-first) or tap a cell then a digit (cell-first); both work everywhere. Pencil-mark mode is a toggle on the strip. Focus Mode is what happens when you pick a digit with no cell selected.
5. **Modes are visible.** No Assist, pencil-mark mode, and a running what-if search each show a labelled state, not a coloured icon.

### 5.2 One layout, three arrangements

**Wide (about 1200px and up, any pointer).** Two columns. Left: header strip (puzzle name, shelf, progress, errors), board, digit strip. Right: the lesson panel, which contains the hint card at the top and a collapsed "Techniques" section under it. The right column never scrolls past the fold for the hint card at 768px tall: the hierarchy is collapsed by default and opens below the card. Auto-Solve and Keyboard Shortcuts leave the column (see 5.5).

**Medium (about 800px to 1200px, mouse or touch, so laptops with a narrow window and tablets in landscape).** Same two columns with a smaller board and a narrower lesson panel. If the width does not allow both, the lesson panel becomes a side sheet that opens on Hint and can be pinned open.

**Stacked (under about 800px, and tablets in portrait).** One column: header strip, board, digit strip. Hint is a button on the strip. A hint opens a bottom sheet over the lower half; the board scrolls or shrinks so the hint's cells stay visible above the sheet. The sheet has the same Look / Why / What to do, the same Beginner/Expert switch, Apply and "Next hint". On a tablet in portrait the board is capped at about 600px so the digit strip stays under the thumbs.

### 5.3 The hint card, designed by state

| State | What the player sees |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | One line: "Stuck? Ask for a hint." with the Hint button. No lightbulb art. |
| Nudge (graduated hint, roadmap) | "Look at box 4" and the box outlined on the board. Buttons: "Show me the step", "I found it". |
| Found | Technique name and tier as the heading. The digits involved as chips (all of them, not the first). Look / Why / What to do. Apply and "Next hint". The legend names a cell from this hint. |
| What-if | A different heading and colour: "No named technique here. Reasoning by trial." The chain replay under it. Same buttons. |
| Searching | "Looking for a what-if chain..." with Cancel, in the same card. |
| No Assist | The card is replaced by the timer and "Hints are off. Turn No Assist off to ask." |
| Solved | Techniques this puzzle used, with the ones the player applied themselves marked. Time and errors. "Next puzzle on this shelf" and "Try the shelf above". |
| Nothing left | "Every remaining cell is a single. You can finish this one yourself." |

### 5.4 The header

Left: puzzle name and shelf, tappable to open the library. Centre or under the board: progress and errors on every width (the phone strip, everywhere). Right: Hint on touch devices (on desktop it lives in the panel and on H), No Assist as a labelled switch, Load puzzle, Sign in, and one overflow menu for print, copy, colours, about and shortcuts.

### 5.5 What moves and what goes

- **Technique hierarchy** becomes "Techniques" inside the lesson panel, collapsed by default, remembered per player. Counts stay; Scan and Search stay in it.
- **Auto-Solve** moves into Techniques as "Watch the mentor solve" with one speed control. It is a demonstration, not a play control.
- **Keyboard shortcuts** move to the overflow menu and a "?" key.
- **Focus Mode** is absorbed by the digit strip: pick a digit with no cell selected and the board highlights it, with the placed count on the digit. The card goes.
- **Colour settings** stay but move behind the overflow menu, reduced to a handful of complete themes (default dark, high-contrast, paper light) with the custom hex option kept for players who want it.
- **The tour** becomes three inline prompts: one on the board ("Tap a cell, then a digit"), one on the Hint button ("Stuck? Ask"), one on the first hint card ("Apply it, or place the digit yourself"). No modal on arrival.
- **Toasts** move to the bottom on desktop and above the strip on phones, never over the board. "Resumed your puzzle" goes; the puzzle name in the header says the same thing.
- **Library** rows gain: the techniques a puzzle teaches (from the analyser), a done tick with best time when signed in, and "Continue" on the puzzle in progress. Rename "Nightmare" and "X-Wing Territory" on the Easy shelf.
- **Completion** gains the learning summary and the next-puzzle actions above.

### 5.6 Phone specifics

- Hints, the technique reference, what-if and pencil marks exist on the phone, through the bottom sheet.
- Pencil marks fill on load, as on desktop, with a strip toggle to hide them.
- Digit buttons at 44px minimum; nine fit at 360px with 4px gaps.
- After a rejected digit, the digit stays selected but the strip shows "2 is wrong here" for a moment so the player knows why.
- Undo, Erase, Pencil, Hint on the strip as labelled buttons. Clear (wipe) only in the overflow menu, with a confirm.
- No Assist is a switch in the overflow menu, off by default, with the timer visible when on. Today's "always No Assist" on the phone is dropped once hints exist there.

### 5.7 Visual language

Keep the dark board. Reduce the chrome: one card style, one accent colour for actions (blue), one for hint pattern cells (blue tint), one for eliminations (red tint), one for the digit to place (amber), and reserve green for "solved" only. Drop gradients and glows on buttons. Body text at 16px on every surface; the explanation is the thing people read.

## 6. Decisions for the founder

1. **One page or two?** The proposal merges the desktop and mobile pages into one adaptive page. This is the biggest change and the one that makes tablets and phones teach. Alternative: keep two pages and bring hints to the mobile page as a bottom sheet (the current roadmap item).
2. **Light theme?** Many people solve on paper in daylight. A "paper" theme is cheap once colours are centralised. Yes or later.
3. **Colour customisation.** Keep the full editor behind the menu, reduce it to themes, or drop it.
4. **Auto-Solve.** Keep as a demonstration inside Techniques, or remove from the player product and keep it for testing only.
5. **The tour.** Inline prompts as proposed, or keep the modal.
6. **No Assist on the phone.** Keep the phone always-timed until hints arrive there, or switch to opt-in No Assist now.

## 7. Suggested order of work

1. **Lesson first, on every width.** Reorder the panel, collapse the hierarchy by default, absorb Focus Mode into a digit strip under the board, move Auto-Solve and Shortcuts out of the column. Fixes conclusions 1 and 3 on desktop, laptop and tablet landscape without touching the phone.
2. **Hint card states.** The table in 5.3, including the what-if heading, all-digit chips and the per-hint legend. Fixes conclusion 4 and the honesty items from the product review.
3. **One adaptive page.** The stacked arrangement with the hint bottom sheet replaces the mobile page. Brings hints, pencil marks and the reference to phones and portrait tablets. Fixes conclusion 2.
4. **Header, tour, library, completion.** The overflow menu, inline prompts, library rows with techniques and continue, the learning summary on completion.

Each phase is a spec of its own once the decisions above are made.
